# VO TEST SCRIPT
### *A Corpse in Good Standing* — casting / register proof
Runtime target: ~3 minutes. Not a demo reel. A stress test.

---

## PURPOSE

This script exists to answer three questions:

1. **Is Corvin castable?** He has to be funny and dying at the same time, in the same line, for fifteen hours. Most actors can do one.
2. **Does Sabine work without warmth?** She never apologizes and never softens. If she reads as cold rather than *controlled*, the romance dies and the whole third act goes with it.
3. **Can the voice survive the boring lines?** Scene D is the real test. Anyone can land a good monologue. Adventure-game VO is 80% "I can't reach that."

Record A, B, C in order. Then record D. **D is the one that decides it.**

---

## VOCAL DIRECTION

**CORVIN VALE** — mid-thirties, dry, fast. The reference is a man telling a joke at his own wake because if he stops talking he'll have to feel something. Not gravel-voiced. Not Batman. He was a clerk, not a pirate.
Critical note: **no wetness in the voice.** The instinct will be to play "drowned" with a rasp or a gurgle. Wrong. He sounds completely normal, which is what makes him unsettling. The only tell is that he never quite breathes in the right places — he doesn't need to, and he's forgotten to fake it.

**SABINE CROIX** — forties. Low, unhurried, never raises. She's the most powerful person on the island and has nothing to prove to anyone in the room. Plays *amused* far more than *angry*. When she finally does something tender in Scene C, she does it with her hands and says nothing — the actor has to hold silence for four full seconds and not fill it.

**OSSUARY KANE** — ancient, courteous, calcified from the ribs down. Warm. **This is the direction people get wrong.** He is not a growling monster. He's a good recruiter — patient, reasonable, genuinely likeable. The horror is that his offer is *good*. If the actor plays menace, recast.

## SCENE A — "Wet"
*Sabine's office, Act I close. Corvin has just walked in for the first time since drowning. He is dripping on a very expensive carpet.*

**SABINE:** You're dripping on the Persian.

**CORVIN:** It's brine. It's antiquing. You'll thank me in a decade.

**SABINE:** *(not looking up)* You always did know how to make an entrance.

**CORVIN:** I drowned, Sabine.

**SABINE:** Yes. It was very dramatic. I cried for almost a full minute.

**CORVIN:** A minute.

**SABINE:** It was a busy week.

*(beat)*

**CORVIN:** I need my name back on the roll.

**SABINE:** You need a great many things. Your name's about fourth.

**CORVIN:** What's first?

**SABINE:** A reason for me to care, and you've never once led with one.

> **Direction:** Play the whole scene as two people picking up a conversation from eight days ago. No exposition voice. Neither of them explains anything to the other, because neither of them needs to — the audience is eavesdropping, not being briefed.

---

## SCENE B — "The Offer"
*Kane's parlour, Act II. Kane is seated. He does not stand for anyone; his legs are coral.*

**KANE:** Sit down, Mr. Vale. You've got six days and you're spending them standing up in other men's rooms.

**CORVIN:** I'll stand.

**KANE:** Of course you will. *(pause)* Nine days. You'll spend six of them being clever and three of them being frightened. I'm offering you the alternative, and you're going to insult me instead, because you think that's the same thing as having a choice.

**CORVIN:** It's adjacent.

**KANE:** It's cheaper. *(warmly)* I read your manifest, by the way. The Kestrel. Eleven in the hold.

**CORVIN:** *(nothing)*

**KANE:** I'm not threatening you with it. Everyone threatens you with it — that's why it stopped working. I'm telling you I read it and I'd like you to work for me anyway. *(beat)* You've spent your whole life waiting for someone to know the worst thing and stay in the room. Here I am. Sitting down.

> **Direction:** Kane's last three lines are the most dangerous writing in the game and must be played with genuine kindness. If there's a single note of gloating, it's a villain speech. If it's warm, it's a job offer, and the player will feel themselves want to take it. That's the scene.

---

## SCENE C — "The Wrist"
*Sabine's office, later. She crosses to him. Takes his wrist. Checks for a pulse.*

**SABINE:** Hold still.

*(She presses two fingers to the inside of his wrist. Four full seconds of silence. There is no pulse. She does not remove her hand.)*

**CORVIN:** *(quietly)* That's not how you check.

**SABINE:** I know how to check.

*(Two more seconds. She lets go.)*

**SABINE:** Get out of my office, Corvin.

**CORVIN:** *(going)* Right.

> **Direction:** The silence is the scene. Do not fill it with breath, fabric, or a sigh — the room tone carries it. Sabine's last line is not a rejection and must not be played as one; it's the only way she has of ending something she can't be in the middle of. Corvin's "Right" is an entire relationship. One syllable. Don't decorate it.

---

## SCENE D — HOTSPOT SAMPLER
*The real test. These are the lines an actor says four hundred times over a playthrough. Record them cold, no warm-up, in one pass.*

**Look at, generic:**
1. "It's a door. I've had complicated relationships with less."
2. "Ledger. Somebody's been honest in it, which is a first for this island."
3. "A rope. Load-bearing. Unlike me."

**Use, failed:**
4. "No."
5. "That's not going to work and I think we both knew that."
6. "I've been dead three days and that's still the worst idea I've had."

**Talk to, nobody there:**
7. "I'm talking to a bollard. He's better company than most."

**Inventory, Act III degraded:**
8. "Something metal. I think it mattered."

**Self-examination:**
9. "There's salt in the knuckles now. It grows at night. I've started checking it the way other men check a watch."

> **Direction:** Lines 4 and 5 are the audition. A failed-use line is the actor's enemy — it's a refusal, it's repetitive, and it's the player being told no. If those two land dry and land *funny*, the actor can carry fifteen hours. If they sound like an apology, they can't.
> Line 8 is the whole third act in six words. It should be said exactly as flatly as lines 1–3, because Corvin doesn't know anything's wrong yet.

---

## RECORDING NOTES

- **Room:** dry, close, minimal reverb. Kane's scenes get a slight stone tail added in post — never performed.
- **No processing on Corvin.** Any "dead voice" effect is a mistake. If it's tempting, the performance isn't working.
- **Sabine sits low in the mix** and slightly further from the mic than Corvin in every shared scene. He's always leaning in. She never is.
- Record Scene D **first** in any callback. It's the disqualifier; there's no point doing Scene C with someone who can't say "No" interestingly.

---

## COST REALITY — READ BEFORE COMMITTING

This test costs almost nothing. Full VO does not. Rough scope from the story bible: 40–60k words of dialogue, 96 duel attack lines, 62 confessions each with an elaboration, plus full hotspot coverage across every room.

For a solo dev that's the single largest line item in the project — larger than art. Options, honestly:

1. **Full VO** — best result, highest cost, and it hard-locks the script. Every rewrite becomes a re-record.
2. **Partial VO** — voice the duels and the Sabine/Kane scenes only, text elsewhere. Gets you 80% of the emotional payload for maybe 20% of the lines. This is what I'd recommend.
3. **Text only** — costs nothing, ships faster, and *A Corpse in Good Standing* would be in extremely good company. Monkey Island 1 had no voice at all.

Option 2 also protects the script: you can keep rewriting hotspot lines up to ship, because nobody has to say them.
