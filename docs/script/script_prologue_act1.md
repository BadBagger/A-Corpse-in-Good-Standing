# A CORPSE IN GOOD STANDING
## PROLOGUE — "WET"
Full script. Act 0, opening of game.

---

**FADE IN — BLACK. Water sounds. Something dragging.**

**CORVIN (V.O.):** The tide comes in twice a day at Mordida. Once with the fish. Once with everybody else.

---

### 1. THE MUDFLATS — DAWN

*Grey light. A body face-down in the silt. It coughs. Keeps coughing. Sits up.*

*CORVIN VALE, mid-thirties, in a wool coat so waterlogged it's black. Barefoot. He looks at his own hands for a while.*

**CORVIN:** Huh.

*He stands. Sways. Doesn't fall.*

**CORVIN:** That's a Tuesday.

*He pats his coat. Pockets. Inside pocket. Nothing.*

**CORVIN:** No boots. No purse. No — *(checks watch pocket)* — right. Well. Somebody had a lovely morning.

*He looks out at the harbor. The leviathan ribs rise out of the water in the distance, lamps strung between them.*

**CORVIN:** I know that. Why do I know that and not — *(stops)* — what day is it.

*Silence.*

**CORVIN:** No, that's fine. That's fine, people forget days.

---

### 2. THE OLD QUAY

*Rotting boardwalk. Mooring posts along the edge, each one grey and vaguely person-shaped. Corvin walks past three of them. The fourth one talks.*

**BOLLARD:** Corvin?

*Corvin keeps walking. Stops. Walks back.*

**BOLLARD:** Corvin Vale.

**CORVIN:** ...Tomas?

**BOLLARD:** Oh, thank Christ. I've been trying to get someone's attention for two years and everyone just ties a rope to me.

**CORVIN:** Tomas, you're a bollard.

**BOLLARD:** I'm aware.

**CORVIN:** You're a *bollard*, Tomas.

**BOLLARD:** Yes, and you're standing in mud with no shoes on, so let's both take a moment before we start ranking each other.

*Corvin crouches down to eye level. There are eyes. Sort of.*

**CORVIN:** How long?

**BOLLARD:** Two years, give or take. I ran out of days and the salt got the rest. It's not so bad. You'd be amazed what people say in front of a mooring post.

**CORVIN:** Ran out of days.

**BOLLARD:** *(carefully)* Corvin. When did you get out of the water?

**CORVIN:** I didn't get *in* the water.

**BOLLARD:** That's not what I asked.

*Beat.*

**CORVIN:** This morning.

**BOLLARD:** Right.

**CORVIN:** Tomas.

**BOLLARD:** Put your hand on your chest.

**CORVIN:** Tomas—

**BOLLARD:** Put your hand on your chest, Corvin.

*He does. Waits. Moves it slightly. Waits.*

*He takes his hand away and looks at it like it's the hand's fault.*

**CORVIN:** That's — no, that's a technique thing, you have to—

**BOLLARD:** Corvin.

**CORVIN:** I've never been good at finding it, even when—

**BOLLARD:** *Corvin.*

*Long pause. Water slapping the pilings.*

**CORVIN:** How long do I get?

**BOLLARD:** Nine days from when you went under. How many have you lost?

**CORVIN:** I don't know.

**BOLLARD:** Then find out. That's the first thing. Everything else is second.

---

### 3. THE OLD QUAY — CONTINUOUS

**CORVIN:** What's it like? The end of it.

**BOLLARD:** You want the honest answer or the one that helps?

**CORVIN:** Honest.

**BOLLARD:** The memory goes first. Oldest to newest, like lamps going out down a street. You lose your mother, then your childhood, then the last twenty years, and the last thing left is whatever happened to you five minutes ago, and then you're standing very still and someone's tying a rope to you.

**CORVIN:** ...And the one that helps?

**BOLLARD:** It's quick at the end.

**CORVIN:** Is that true?

**BOLLARD:** No.

**CORVIN:** *(standing)* Great. Good talk.

**BOLLARD:** Corvin.

**CORVIN:** What.

**BOLLARD:** Somebody put you in there.

**CORVIN:** I know.

**BOLLARD:** Nobody drowns in Mordida harbor by accident. It's four feet deep at the quay and you could swim before you could read.

**CORVIN:** I *know*, Tomas.

**BOLLARD:** Then go find out who, while you've still got the equipment to be angry with.

*Corvin starts up the boardwalk. Stops.*

**CORVIN:** Tomas — do you remember me? Properly? All of it?

**BOLLARD:** *(pause)* I remember I was glad to see you.

**CORVIN:** That's not the same thing.

**BOLLARD:** No. It's better. Go on.

---

### 4. SALT MARKET — MORNING

*A crowded street under whale-oil lamps. Fishmongers, a Church confession stall with a queue outside it, a man selling boots. Corvin heads straight for the boots.*

**BOOT SELLER:** Morning. Size?

**CORVIN:** Eleven. And credit.

**BOOT SELLER:** Credit's for people I know.

**CORVIN:** You know me. Vale. I did your brother's transfer papers.

*The seller looks up. Looks properly. His face does something complicated.*

**BOOT SELLER:** ...Vale.

**CORVIN:** There we are.

**BOOT SELLER:** Vale's dead.

**CORVIN:** Well. That's the sort of thing people say.

**BOOT SELLER:** No — *no* — they pulled you out Thursday. I *saw* it. They laid you on the ice at the fish hall, everyone came through, I *stood in the line*—

**CORVIN:** Right, and I appreciate—

**BOOT SELLER:** — I brought my *daughter*—

**CORVIN:** That seems like a choice.

*The seller backs into his own table. Boots everywhere. People turning.*

**CORVIN:** Look. Nobody needs to—

*A woman in the confession queue turns around, sees him, and screams. Properly screams. The whole street stops.*

*Corvin stands in the middle of it, dripping, barefoot, entirely still.*

**CORVIN:** *(to nobody)* Thursday.

*He does the arithmetic on his fingers. It doesn't take long.*

**CORVIN:** Six days.

*He picks up a pair of boots off the ground, unhurried, and walks out through the crowd, which parts for him without anyone deciding to.*

**CORVIN (V.O.):** Six days to find out who held me under.

*He glances back at the confession stall. The Church sign above it: THE DROWNED CANNOT LIE — ONE SHILLING.*

**CORVIN (V.O.):** And I'd just discovered I couldn't lie about anything.

**CORVIN (V.O.):** Which, given my line of work, was going to be a problem.

**CUT TO TITLE.**

## SCENE 5 — "WET" (Sabine's office, end of Act I)

*Corvin enters. Water runs off him onto a very good carpet. SABINE CROIX doesn't look up from her ledger.*

**SABINE:** You're dripping on the Persian.

**CORVIN:** It's brine. It's antiquing. You'll thank me in a decade.

**SABINE:** You always did know how to make an entrance.

**CORVIN:** I drowned, Sabine.

**SABINE:** Yes. It was very dramatic. I cried for almost a full minute.

**CORVIN:** A minute.

**SABINE:** It was a busy week.

*She writes another line. Sets the pen down. Looks at him for the first time.*

**SABINE:** You're on the roll again. I saw.

**CORVIN:** I had to out-confess a seventy-year-old woman in front of a paying audience.

**SABINE:** How was it?

**CORVIN:** Humiliating and extremely well attended.

**SABINE:** What did you give her?

**CORVIN:** *(beat)* The Kestrel.

*That lands. She sits back.*

**SABINE:** Out loud.

**CORVIN:** Out loud, in a hall, to about forty people and a man selling pastries.

**SABINE:** Corvin—

**CORVIN:** It's fine. It's actually — it's the only clever thing I've done since Tuesday. Nobody can hold it over me now. You can't blackmail a man with a thing he shouted.

**SABINE:** That's not why you did it.

**CORVIN:** No.

**SABINE:** Why did you do it?

**CORVIN:** Because I wanted one person on this island to know the worst of it and still be standing there afterwards. *(beat)* She was. She wrote my name back in. Didn't say anything about it.

*Silence. Sabine gets up. Crosses to him. Water pooling on her floor and she walks straight through it.*

**SABINE:** Hold still.

**CORVIN:** What—

**SABINE:** Hold *still*.

*She takes his wrist. Presses two fingers to the inside of it.*

*Four seconds. Nothing.*

*She doesn't take her hand away.*

**CORVIN:** *(quietly)* That's not how you check.

**SABINE:** I know how to check.

*Two more seconds. She lets go. Goes back to her desk. Picks up the pen.*

**SABINE:** Get out of my office, Corvin.

**CORVIN:** Right.

*He goes. At the door—*

**SABINE:** Six days.

**CORVIN:** Five.

*He leaves. She sits with the pen in her hand and doesn't write anything for a long time.*

**END OF ACT I.**
